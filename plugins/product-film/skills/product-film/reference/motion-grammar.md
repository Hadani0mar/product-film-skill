# Motion Grammar: coherent movement across the whole film

A motion grammar is a small set of repeatable physical rules that makes every shot feel authored by the same director.

## Build a grammar before the storyboard

Choose 2–4 primary behaviors:
- **travel** — one object carries meaning between states;
- **expand/collapse** — surfaces become environments or return to UI;
- **snap/settle** — precise product-like placement;
- **track/orbit** — camera follows a task or hero;
- **mask/reveal** — product geometry controls visibility;
- **type handoff** — copy transforms into product UI;
- **pulse/propagate** — data or status travels through a system.

Then choose only 1–2 accent behaviors:
- whip;
- chromatic hit;
- lens warp;
- light streak;
- pixel/dissolve;
- 3D depth burst.

Accent behavior must never become the base grammar.

## Motion sentence

Every important move should have:
1. **anticipation** — cue the viewer;
2. **action** — one readable transformation;
3. **settle** — visual confirmation;
4. **hold** — enough time to understand;
5. **handoff** — next scene inherits a position, vector, object, mask, or idea.

If a move has action but no settle/hold, it often feels cheap or rushed.

## Kinetic typography rules

- Animate semantic chunks, not arbitrary letters by default.
- Preserve stable line boxes unless the concept explicitly uses reflow.
- One phrase gets one dominant motion idea.
- Use weight, scale, position, tracking, mask, and opacity in a controlled hierarchy.
- Typography can lead into UI, but should not compete with active UI.
- A large statement should typically finish before the next product action begins.

## UI animation rules

- Respect interaction causality: cursor/touch/action first, response second.
- Real product state transitions beat invented decorative transitions.
- Preserve component geometry whenever possible.
- Do not animate every element on screen.
- Use stagger only when order communicates hierarchy.
- Loading, success, error, and notification states should follow product truth.

## Camera grammar

Limit the camera to a small vocabulary per film.
- Push: discovery/importance.
- Pull: system/scale.
- Track: workflow.
- Orbit: hero dimensionality.
- Parallax: depth.
- Macro: precision.
- Whip: editorial punctuation.

Never use camera movement to compensate for weak staging.

## Temporal grammar

For a 60fps timeline:
- micro action: ~6–16f
- UI action: ~12–30f
- meaningful settle: ~8–24f
- readable hold: ~18–72f depending on complexity
- hero reveal: often 1.5–4s
- CTA/logo close: hold long enough to read without pausing

These are starting ranges, not hard presets. Distance, scale, easing, and audio matter more than raw duration.

## Beat mapping

Map beats to event classes:
- **primary beat** → structural cut, hero action, major copy change;
- **secondary beat** → support motion, icon, data accent;
- **micro rhythm** → small UI response, cursor, detail.

Do not slam the whole camera on every beat.

## Density rule

At any moment:
- one hero event;
- at most two supporting events;
- atmosphere stays subordinate.

If three independent focal animations happen together, simplify.
